# Research overview

## Research question

Which career paths connected to artificial intelligence and related technologies are growing, what do workers in those roles do, and what skills and routes can help students prepare?

## Early findings

Several signals point to demand across the AI ecosystem, but they measure different things:

- **U.S. occupational projections:** The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects data scientist employment to grow **33.5% from 2024 to 2034** (about 82,500 additional jobs). Information security analysts are projected at **28.5%**, and software developers at **15.8%** over the same period. These are occupation-level projections, not counts of jobs with “AI” in their title. [BLS, July 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/artificial-intelligence-information-technology-and-employment-2024-34.htm)
- **Global employer expectations:** The World Economic Forum lists AI and machine learning specialists and big data specialists among the fastest-growing roles expected by surveyed employers through 2030. This is a survey-based global outlook, not a U.S. government employment forecast. [WEF, Future of Jobs Report 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/2-jobs-outlook)
- **Career descriptions:** Titles vary across employers. “AI engineer,” “ML engineer,” and “AI specialist” can describe overlapping but different work. We will compare actual duties and skills in job postings before drawing conclusions.

## Career areas to investigate

| Career area | Typical work to investigate | Why it belongs in the project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI / machine learning specialist | Build, evaluate, and deploy models or AI-enabled systems | Directly connected to AI development; highlighted in employer outlooks |
| Data scientist | Analyze data, build statistical or machine-learning models, communicate findings | Strong BLS growth projection; skills transfer across industries |
| AI product management | Translate user and business needs into AI product requirements; coordinate delivery and evaluation | Connects technical teams with customers and responsible product decisions |
| AI governance and responsible AI | Document risks, test controls, support policy, privacy, and accountability | Growing organizational need as AI systems are deployed |
| AI security | Protect models, data, and AI-enabled products; assess new attack surfaces | Bridges AI with established cybersecurity work |

*The last three are emerging or cross-functional job families. Their exact titles and growth rates vary; we will validate them with current postings and occupational sources.*

## Method

1. **Collect evidence:** Use government labor data, occupational databases, employer reports, and a dated sample of job postings.
2. **Normalize titles:** Group similar titles by work performed rather than assuming title wording is consistent.
3. **Compare roles:** Record core tasks, tools, skills, education or experience requirements, and entry routes.
4. **Check growth claims:** Note the geography, time period, and method behind each number. Separate projections from survey expectations and job-posting counts.
5. **Update the portfolio:** Add sources and a date to each research note so findings can be revisited.

## Live job sample

I reviewed current public postings across New York and New Jersey and grouped them by career stage and job family. Examples include Summer 2027 research internships, associate AI engineering and forward-deployed roles, mid-level AI engineering, senior AI security, and model operations roles. See the [NY / NJ job market snapshot](/research/job-market-snapshot) for exact titles, employer links, paraphrased duties, stated requirements, and notes about posting status.

- Which titles are genuinely new, and which describe existing work changed by AI?
