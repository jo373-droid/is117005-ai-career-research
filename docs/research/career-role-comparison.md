# Career research: two employer descriptions

**Research question (Nov. 12, 2026):** What work do two New York area employers describe for early-career AI engineering, what preparation do they ask for, and what evidence should a student portfolio show?

**Method:** I compared two original employer career pages, recording what each page states and what it does not state. I checked the pages on Nov. 12, 2026. These are two examples, not a representative labor-market sample.

## Role card 1 — American Express

**Role / location:** Campus Undergraduate Full-Time Engineer — 2027 AI Engineer I, Enterprise Technology Services; New York, NY; hybrid.  
**Original source:** [American Express career posting](https://egug.fa.us2.oraclecloud.com/hcmUI/CandidateExperience/en/sites/CX_1/job/26012982), posted Sept. 1, 2026; checked Nov. 12, 2026.  
**Availability check:** The career page was accessible and showed an application page/button on Nov. 12. This is an observed page state, not a guarantee that applications remain open later.  
**Work described:** Assist with AI/ML and LLM integration, data preparation and validation, debugging, evaluation, documentation, and collaboration with engineering, product, data, security, risk, and business teams. The role includes reliability, safety, governance, security, and compliance concerns.  
**Eligibility / preparation stated:** It is labeled a campus undergraduate full-time 2027 position. The page says U.S. work eligibility is required and AmEx will not pursue visa sponsorship. Check the complete live posting for any degree, graduation, or location details before treating yourself as eligible.  
**Deadline wording:** No application deadline was stated in the page content reviewed.  
**Evidence boundary:** The task list and sponsorship statement are facts from this posting. Inferring that the position is suitable for a particular student requires checking that student's circumstances privately; do not publish those details.

## Comparison and research finding

Both descriptions connect AI work to software and real-world workflows. AmEx gives a broad enterprise AI engineering scope with explicit cross-functional collaboration and responsible-use concerns; Palantir emphasizes agent infrastructure, permissions, evaluations, and operational reliability. The AmEx posting provides a dated, detailed task list and an explicit sponsorship constraint. Palantir's accessible description suggests hands-on agent systems work, but source-access limits leave qualifications, deadline, and application status uncertain. Two postings cannot establish which career is growing faster or how common these duties are.

**Assumptions to test:** A public portfolio demonstrating one small AI-enabled workflow, its evaluation, and clear limits may help a student show relevant skills. The postings do not say that a portfolio is required or guarantees an interview.

## Proposed portfolio feature and check

**Decision:** Add a research-to-role comparison card that separates employer-stated tasks and eligibility from student interpretation, and visibly records source date, deadline wording, and availability-check status. This makes the evidence inspectable and prevents missing information from appearing as fact.

**Why this feature follows from the evidence:** AmEx explicitly names documentation, evaluation, governance, and reliability; Palantir names agent interfaces, permissions, and evaluations. Both make it useful to show a small project with a test and stated limitation. Because Palantir's qualifications and active application state could not be confirmed through the reader, the card must label those fields “unverified,” not fill them with assumptions.
